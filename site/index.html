A timeline that fits how you remember

Life doesn’t come in exact dates and tidy lists. Chronicle is built for the way memories really are: overlapping, approximate, and far more interesting next to each other.

Everything side by side

One row per part of your life — homes, school, jobs, relationships, a hobby — and one group per person. Put your parents’ lives next to yours and see who was where when you were born, or what your dad was doing at your age.

  • Pan and zoom from a century down to a day
  • Groups nest as deep as your family does, and fold away into one row
  • Ages computed from birth dates; the years before someone was born are shaded
  • A mini-map, search, filters, and hiding what you don’t need right now

Honest about fuzzy memories

“Sometime in the 90s”, “around 2009”, “June 2018” — every date knows how sure it is, and the bar shows it: the vaguer the date, the softer the edge. Relationships can fade in, jobs can still be going on.

  • Exact, day, month, year or circa — per date
  • Spans for the long things, moments for the milestones — a wedding, a first marathon
  • Places, descriptions, links — and a favicon instead of an emoji where there’s a website

A conversation, not a blank page

The first time you open Chronicle, it asks one thing per screen: when you were born, where you lived, learned and worked, who you were with, your kids. Drag the dots to roughly when — the preview draws your life as you answer, and every row is there to edit, whenever you like.

  • Place names looked up as you type
  • Adding an entry asks what it was, when it started and how long it lasted
  • Skip anything; come back to it later

Made for your phone — not squeezed onto it

On a phone, Chronicle is a full-screen timeline with a mini-map on top and a sheet you pull up from the bottom — built for thumbs, not a shrunken desktop.

  • Pinch and drag the timeline; tap a bar to open it
  • A date editor with two handles on one lane
  • Add an entry or a moment from one button

An account without a password

When you want your timelines on your other devices, create an account with a passkey: Face ID, a fingerprint or your device PIN. Nothing to invent, nothing to forget, nothing anyone can phish.

  • Sign in without typing anything — your device knows the passkey
  • Your phone or password manager keeps it in sync across your devices
  • Prefer a password? That works too, and you can have both
  • No email address is ever asked for

Private by default — and honest about it

A timeline of your life is about as personal as data gets. Here is exactly what happens to it.

Without an account
Everything stays in your browser. Chronicle makes no requests to its server at all — no analytics, no ads, no tracking.
With an account
Your timelines are stored on Chronicle’s server so every device you sign in on has them. Nobody else can see any of them until you share them.
When you share
Viewers only ever see timelines you have published, in the groups you shared with them. Editors see the one group they were invited to, and nothing above or beside it.
What we can’t promise
Stored data is not end-to-end encrypted — the server can read it. And sharing can’t be taken back: stopping a share ends future access, but anyone who already saw something may have kept a copy. The app says both, at the moment it matters.
Leaving
Export everything as a file whenever you like. Deleting your account removes every timeline you own from the server, for everyone.
Open source
Every line of the app and the server is on GitHub — including the one function that decides who may see what.

Questions

Do I need an account?

No. Chronicle works fully without one, and keeps everything in your browser. An account only adds your other devices and the people you share with — and you can create one later: the timelines already on your device come with you.

What does it cost?

Nothing. There are no ads and no paid tier.

What if I forget my password?

There is no email on file, so a password can’t be reset — which is why Chronicle suggests a passkey instead: it’s kept by your phone or password manager. Another signed-in device, or an exported file, is your backup.

Who can see my timelines?

Only you, until you share. Then: the people you shared with — viewers see the timelines you’ve published, editors see the one group they were invited to. A public link shows the published part of one group to anyone holding it, until you switch it off.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything is saved on your device first. Signed in, your changes wait there and are sent the moment you’re back online; other people’s changes arrive then too.

What happens when two people edit the same entry?

Both changes survive as long as they touch different fields — one of you fixes the dates while the other writes the description. If you both change the very same field at the same moment, the later change wins, which is why Chronicle shows who else has an entry open.

Can I get my data out?

Any time: Data → Export JSON downloads every timeline you own. It imports into any other copy of Chronicle.
